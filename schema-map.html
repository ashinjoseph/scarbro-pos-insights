What your POS database actually stores

A map of every table and column that holds data, and — more usefully — which ones are empty. The POS ships as a full retail ERP; the store uses a narrow slice of it. Knowing which slice tells you exactly which questions are answerable today and which need a settings change first.

The shape of it

88 tables exist. Only 26 hold any data at all. The other 62 are the parts of the product you don't use — purchasing, suppliers, warehouses, loyalty, expenses, banking, journals, credit customers.

Why this matters more than the column list

A column existing is not the same as a column holding data. Several of the most valuable-sounding fields in this database — cost price, cashier id, customer id, till variance — are present in the schema and empty in practice. The colour coding throughout this page is fill rate, not existence: full is populated on nearly every row, partial on some, and struck-through means never populated on any row.

Questions this data can answer today

Everything below is supported by columns that are actually populated. The first four are already built into your dashboard; the rest are available and unused.

  • ✓
    When does the store make money?
    Hour, weekday and week-by-week revenue, footfall and basket size. From InvoiceInfo.InvoiceDate + GrandTotal, which are populated on all 17,718 sales. This is the strongest data you have.
  • ✓
    What sells, and what is dying?
    Product and category performance over time, velocity, dead stock, first/last sale date. From Invoice_Product joined to Product.Category.
  • ✓
    How do people pay?
    Cash, debit, credit split by month. From Invoice_Payment. Remember Amount is cash tendered, so subtract InvoiceInfo.Change before treating it as revenue.
  • ✓
    What is being voided?
    1,970 voided bills with timestamps and values, from DeletedInvoices. Enough to spot patterns by hour, day and shift — but not by person (see below).
  • +
    What does basket composition look like?
    Which categories appear together in the same bill, and what a basket containing X is worth versus one without. Fully supported by Invoice_Product and not yet used beyond the lottery attach-rate work.
  • +
    How much of turnover is tax, and on what?
    VAT and VATPer are filled on 48% of lines, and Category.TaxApplicable plus TaxnameX define which categories should be taxed. Comparing the two would find products flagged wrongly at the till.
  • +
    What does card acceptance cost, and how is the mix moving?
    CardProcessing links 658 sales to card transactions by amount. It won't give you the fee (that column is empty), but combined with your Clover statements it sizes the card-fee base.
  • +
    How often is the drawer opened without a sale?
    Logs records 1,742 direct drawer opens and 4,517 new-transaction clicks with timestamps. A no-sale open rate per hour is a standard loss-prevention measure and it is sitting there unused.
  • +
    Which products get removed from a bill before it completes?
    Logs.Operation records line removals by product name and value — a softer signal than a full void, and one nobody is looking at.

What is never recorded at all

No cardholder data is stored anywhere — a correction to what I told you earlier

I previously flagged InvoiceInfo as holding cardholder fields and treated it as sensitive. Checking the fill rates properly: CardNo, CardHolder, ApprovalCode, CardType, RefNo and TransKey are populated on zero of 17,718 rows. The CardProcessing table holds amounts only. The columns exist because the product supports integrated payment terminals; your card processing goes through Clover, so nothing lands in them. This database carries no card data, and no customer names, addresses, emails or phone numbers either — every one of those columns is empty. That materially lowers the risk of holding and sharing this backup.

Also absent, because the modules are unused: purchase orders and goods receipts, supplier accounts, warehouse and stock transfers, expenses, banking and journals, credit customers, wallets and cashback, loyalty members, and staff clock-out records. 62 empty tables in total, listed at the foot of the dictionary.
