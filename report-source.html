01Executive summary

Sales look flat. They are not flat — they are two significant, opposing movements that happen to cancel. Everything else in this report follows from that.

The finding that matters

Total sales trend +1.1% across the window at p = 0.91 — statistically no trend at all. Underneath, customer visits rose 14.6% (p = 0.015) while the average basket fell 11.5% (p = 0.045). Both clear significance; the total does not, because they very nearly cancel. More people are coming in and each is spending less. Traffic is not the constraint — what happens at the counter is.

One limitation that shapes every conclusion

The window runs mid-April to mid-August — straight into summer. Four months cannot separate a growth trend from a seasonal one. Where a category's rise would be explained by warm weather, this report marks it unproven rather than reporting it as growth. Re-running this against a backup covering autumn would settle it.

02Data, repairs and method

What was analysed, what had to be fixed first, and how the statistics were run.

The source

A SQL Server full backup of RetailPOS_DB taken 19 August 2026 on DESKTOP-VDRD582\SQLEXPRESS. Despite the .bak extension being described as MySQL, it is a Microsoft SQL Server backup (MTF container, engine version 10, compatibility level 100). It was restored on SQL Server 2022 and queried directly. Figures come from InvoiceInfo, Invoice_Product and Invoice_Payment.

A repair was required before any cash figure could be trusted

Three invoices carried mis-keyed cash tendered amounts, the worst being $804,906,004,014.00 against a $20.00 sale. Together they pushed reported cash to $886.9 billion and made every payment report meaningless.

InvoiceCash enteredActual saleDate

The error sat only in the tender field. GrandTotal was correct on all three, so sales figures were never affected — only cash reporting. Tendered has been set equal to the sale amount, and reported cash falls from $886.9 billion to $170,730.74. The repair script selects rows by rule rather than by hardcoded ID, runs inside a transaction with a post-check, and is safe to re-run.

How trends were fitted

Ordinary least squares on whole Sunday–Saturday weeks only. The extract begins on a Wednesday and ends on a Wednesday; including those part-weeks would read as a collapse at both ends. Seventeen whole weeks qualify. Every trend is reported with R² and a two-sided p-value, and "significant" means p < 0.05.

Seventeen weeks is enough to detect a strong trend and not enough to detect a weak one. A "not significant" result here means not proven, not proven flat.

The spread is invoice-level discounts and round-off. Agreement to within 0.1% across three independent paths means the data is internally consistent.

No customer or cardholder data exists in this database

InvoiceInfo declares columns for card and customer detail — CardNo, CardHolder, ApprovalCode, CardType, CustID, EmailID, AddressX — and every one is empty on all 17,718 rows. Card processing runs through Clover, so nothing lands in them. Every figure in this report is an aggregate.

Read the drinks growth with care

Drinks, ready-to-drink and beer are the three categories summer inflates, and they rise over exactly the stretch that runs into summer. Four months cannot tell growth from season. Expanding facings now is reasonable; treating the growth as permanent is not. Book the decision for review in October.

The more reliable signal is tobacco

Tobacco is flat in dollars (+1.3%) and has given up 2.35 points of retail share — from 35.4% to 33.0% — while the shop around it grew. It remains the largest retail category, so this is not urgent, but in a nationally declining category the share number is the one to track, and it argues for planning the replacement of that shelf space rather than defending it.

Lottery is a commission business, not a revenue one

The $95,109 held is not income; nearly all of it is remitted to OLG. At a 5% retailer rate the store earns roughly $7,584 on $151,681 of tickets. So lottery contributes about 5% of what the business actually takes in, while occupying 40% of the sales line and 50% of all counter time. The commission rate used here is indicative — check it against your OLG statements before relying on the figure.

Only 15.7% of lottery customers buy anything else

7,491 visits — 42% of every transaction in four months — bought a ticket and nothing more. The ones who do add goods spend $16.11, more than the $13.60 of a customer who never touches lottery. This is not a low-value crowd; it is an unserved one. Converting one in twenty at the ordinary basket would add roughly $5,100 over a comparable period. The queue is already there, and it is currently worth far less per minute than the rest of the shop.

Late trade is the growth story within beer

Beer sold from 9pm onward rose from 8.8% to 18.1% of beer revenue across the window (+0.36 points per week, p = 0.030). In dollars the gap is starker: late beer grew 89% (p = 0.002) against +31% for beer overall. The late trade is taking share within a growing category.

Average unit price falls from $7.39 before 9pm to $5.88 after — the late trade buys singles, not packs. That is a well-defined place to test a multi-buy offer on 473ml cans, and it speaks directly to the shrinking-basket problem in section 03.

A void rate above 11% of transactions is very high for convenience retail, and with no reason recorded on 98.5% of them, nothing is auditable after the fact. The largest single void is $5,600 — against a largest-ever completed sale of $450. The top 20 voids alone account for 35% of all voided value.

The POS cannot attribute any of this to a person

Every attribution field holds a single shared value: InvoiceInfo.UID is "admin" on all 17,718 sales, Logs.UserID is "admin" on all 28,308 log entries, and DeletedInvoices.Operator is only ever "User" or "admin". Nothing distinguishes one member of staff from another.

Correlating voids with the StoreOps shift roster

Matching each void to whoever held the till at that moment is the only route available. It attributes 68% of voids (the rest fall before StoreOps records begin), and till sessions never overlap, so where a session exists the match is unambiguous. It still says who was on shift, never who pressed void.

This does not point at anyone

The differences are beyond chance (χ², p = 2.8×10⁻¹⁵), but the highest rate on the list belongs to the owner, and the two highest belong to the two admin accounts with by far the smallest samples — consistent with void rate tracking corrections and admin work rather than misconduct. Measured by value, everyone sits in a tight 16.2–20.5% band. The control gap is the finding, not any individual.

The till-variance feature exists and is switched off

Of the 28 columns in POS_ClosingCash, 23 are empty on every row — including the denomination breakdown, ClosingBalance and DifferInCash. The POS has a complete cash-count and variance function that is not in use. This is precisely the gap StoreOps was built to fill in spreadsheets; turning it on would give a second, independent count to reconcile against.

The list that matters.

22,725 products — 96.4% of the catalogue — have never been scanned once. And the concentration among those that do sell is extreme: two products (LOTTO IN and INSTANT) are half of all revenue, twenty are 80%, thirty-four are 90%.

This makes the cost-price problem small

Cost is missing on 94% of what sells, which sounds like a large project. It is not. Entering purchase cost on 34 products would enable real margin reporting on 90% of turnover. That is an hour of work, and it is the single highest-return task available — because margin, not revenue, is what tells you whether beer or tobacco earns its shelf space.

Hygiene

Genuinely clean: every sold product carries a barcode, and no barcode is shared between two products. Two problems do need attention:

  • 18 groups of duplicate product names — the same item entered two or three times as separate records, splitting its sales history. CASH BACK exists twice, Tide three times, Van Dens Earphones three times. Each duplicate makes a product look weaker than it is.
  • Categories drift. Roughly $4,624 of beer sits under Drinks and Deli rather than Beer, which understates the beer category by about 12% and inflates the Drinks growth figure in section 04.

Only 49 of 108 defined categories are in use. Drinks holds 146 products for $12,084 — $83 per product — against Tobacco's 90 products for $48,472, or $539 each. Drinks is where the clutter lives.

10Recommendations

Ordered by return on effort.

1

Enter cost price on the top 34 products

An hour of work that unlocks margin reporting on 90% of turnover. Everything else in this report is revenue and volume; only margin tells you what is worth the shelf. This is the highest-return single task available.

2

Give every staff member their own POS login

Costs nothing and turns every question about voids, discounts and drawer opens from circumstantial into definitive. Without it, no loss-prevention question can be answered properly.

3

Work the lottery queue

7,491 visits bought a ticket and nothing else, and lottery customers who do add goods spend more than everyone else. Put impulse lines where the lottery queue actually stands. Converting one in twenty is worth roughly $5,100 per four months.

4

Make the void reason mandatory, and require a manager code above $50

98.5% of voids carry no reason. A threshold approval would have caught the $5,600 void. Also add a till-side sanity check rejecting any tender more than $500 above the sale total, which prevents a repeat of the mis-keyed cash entries.

5

Treat the shrinking basket as the problem to solve

It is the only significant negative in the data and it is cancelling genuine growth in footfall. Start with the $2–$10 band and the 9pm–11pm singles trade, where one added item changes the basket materially.

6

Clean the catalogue

Merge the 18 duplicate product groups, move the misfiled beer out of Drinks and Deli, and archive — not delete — the 22,725 items that have never sold, so lookups and future analysis stop carrying 96% noise.

7

Turn on the POS till-variance feature

It already exists and is switched off. It would give a second independent cash count to reconcile against StoreOps, which is exactly what you want for cash control.

8

Re-run this analysis after autumn

The drinks, RTD and beer growth cannot be separated from summer on four months of data. A backup covering September to November would settle which of them is real, and would make the trend figures in section 03 considerably more reliable.
